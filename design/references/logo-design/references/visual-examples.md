# Annotated visual examples

These original study notes describe examples inspected during the earlier local
source review. They are synthesis, not quotations or newly verified visual
observations. The reproduced page images are intentionally absent from Git.

Use the [source guide](source-guide.md) to locate optional originals. Each example
below gives the PDF and printed page. Inspect the original page when making a
new visual judgment; the note alone does not constitute that inspection. If the
books are unavailable, use the stated operation as a design exercise and report
the source check as not performed. Develop a different construction for the
client's brief; do not trace or rename these marks.

## 1. Negative space has a specific job

Source: Airey, PDF 39 / printed 26. Ed's Electric.

The space between two electrical forms makes an E. The service association and
initial share one construction. Study how the empty shape reads alongside the
solid shapes. For a new concept, ask whether the secondary reading supports the
primary shape. Reject a hidden-letter puzzle that needs the explanation to work.

## 2. One construction can carry two associations

Source: Airey, PDF 50 / printed 37. French Property Exhibition; Roy Smith.

The flag becomes an open doorway or shutters. The page contrasts this with the
earlier lettering and explains why another French symbol would add clutter.
Study the combined idea, not its colors as a template. Ask what can be removed
without losing the business association.

## 3. Explore constructions before polishing

Source: Airey, PDF 116 / printed 103. Filmaps sketches; Josiah Jost.

Film and mapping associations produce several different arrangements. The rough
drawings leave room to reject an idea cheaply. For a new brief, explore different
relationships and silhouettes. Five palettes applied to one finished shape do
not provide this range.

## 4. Compare different concepts without color

Source: Airey, PDF 125 / printed 112. Woodmere alternatives.

Signature-like and architectural constructions offer different impressions.
Their plain presentation lets the client compare those differences. Use equal
perceived size and finish. Do not make a weak concept look stronger through a
larger mockup or metallic effect.

## 5. Select the shape, then refine its treatment

Source: Airey, PDF 126 / printed 113. Woodmere refinement.

The chosen structural idea receives further refinement and color. Study which
features remain stable across treatments. In new work, confirm that the mark
still identifies itself when the preferred palette is absent.

## 6. Identity includes repeated use

Source: Airey, PDF 128 / printed 115. Kerling; Andrew Sabatier.

The same identity appears across publications and supporting layouts. Study
the relationship between symbol, lettering, palette, and surrounding material.
For a client, choose applications they actually use. A handsome brochure mockup
does not prove a mobile header or stitched uniform works.

## 7. Proportion and empty space distinguish basic forms

Source: Müller, PDF 36 / printed 72-73. Basic Forms.

A small vocabulary produces different silhouettes through arrangement, scale,
cuts, and negative space. This is a visual deduction from the examples. For new
work, change the relationship among shapes before adding ornament. A circle or
diagonal cut alone is not a distinctive concept.

## 8. A stable symbol supports controlled variation

Source: Müller, PDF 47 / printed 102-103. Daiei identity program.

Study the stable symbol across packaging, uniforms, treatments, and the manual.
The accompanying type supports it. A system can vary without inventing a new
logo for each surface. Decide which identifying feature must remain unchanged.

## 9. A monogram must survive a reading check

Source: Müller, PDF 88 / printed 322-323. Two Letters.

Shared stems, enclosure, overlap, and figure-ground changes combine initials.
Study the letter order and the openings that distinguish one letter from another.
An attractive interlock can still read as the wrong initials. Inspect the drawing
without its rationale and again at the intended small size.

## 10. A separate icon is optional

Source: Müller, PDF 93 / printed 358-359. Words.

The lettering itself supplies a recognizable silhouette. Compare rhythm,
counter shapes, connections, and unusual letter construction. A wordmark can
give the name priority. Preserve reading before pushing an identifying detail;
do not add an icon solely because other concepts have one.
