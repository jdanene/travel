# Review and delivery

These are practical checks derived from the books. The pixel sizes below are
modern working examples, not measurements prescribed by either author. Follow
the project's inspection rules, including any limits on screenshots or repeated
rendering; record checks that those rules or available tools leave unperformed.

## Compare the right things

Begin with the identifying structure in one color. Use equal perceived size,
consistent labels, and the same background. Compare color later. A metallic
finish, dramatic sign photograph, or larger canvas can bias a choice toward an
otherwise weaker mark.

For each direction, assess these questions with a short reason:

- **Fit:** Does the visible design support the audience and intended impression?
- **Identity:** What specific feature distinguishes it from the other choices?
- **Reading:** Can the name and intended initials be read without the rationale?
- **Reduction:** Does the feature survive the smallest required use?
- **Coherence:** Do symbol and lettering share compatible weight and character?
- **Reproduction:** Does the design work in its required colors and media?

Use pass, revise, or not checked. Do not total subjective scores into a fictitious
scientific ranking. A failed critical use can outweigh several appealing details.

## Inspect real uses

| Use | Inspect | What to change if it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Small symbol | Try 16, 24, 32, and 64 CSS pixels when those uses are relevant. | Open gaps, reduce detail, or define a simplified small variant. |
| Website lockup | Render at the actual header width on desktop and mobile. | Rebalance symbol and name; simplify or omit the descriptor at a stated breakpoint. |
| Profile avatar | Inspect the site's square and circular crop. | Give the identifying shape enough safe space; use the mark instead of tiny full wording. |
| Vehicle or sign | Inspect the same layout at a distant viewing size. | Improve name hierarchy and stroke weight; remove decorative clutter. |
| Single-color printing | Inspect solid ink and knockout versions. | Remove color-dependent separations or create a deliberate monochrome construction. |
| Embroidery or vinyl | Check fine gaps, joins, small type, and isolated pieces. | Simplify for the intended size; confirm the vendor's limits. |
| Light and dark fields | Inspect the actual positive and reverse variants. | Correct optical weight and counters; do not assume automatic inversion works. |

For final embroidery, inspect a vendor sew-out at the intended size on the
intended fabric. A clean vector or screen mockup does not verify stitching.

Choose sizes from the application. A full business name need not fit in a
16-pixel icon. Identify which variant serves which use instead of treating every
failure as a reason to oversimplify the primary logo.

Inspect empty and filled spaces, not only outlines. Rotate the mark to look for
accidental readings. Check that overlapping initials do not form a different
letter, medical symbol, or unrelated object. The relevant misreading depends on
the brief; do not impose one category's exclusions on all clients.

A recall check requires a person to see, then recall or identify, the mark.
Without that observation, describe the intended memory feature and the designer's
assessment. Do not report that the design has passed a memorability test.

## Competitor comparison

Inspect a small, relevant set from the client's market. Record what was actually
viewed and when. Compare shape, letter construction, composition, and dominant
features, not just palette. If the check has not happened, say so.

A visual search can identify similarities. It does not establish legal clearance.
For a final identity, record the provenance of artwork and fonts. Do not assume
that a stock asset's license permits trademark use or that generated work is
exclusive. Do not invent current legal rules; use authoritative sources if the
user requests registration or legal guidance.

## Useful handoff at each stage

### Written proposal

When imagery is excluded, supply the construction, rationale, and risk. State
that no artwork exists and that visual checks remain open.

### Concept study

Supply the requested alternatives, their rationale and tradeoffs, and a neutral
comparison. Keep the rough exploration when it helps explain the decision. State
which reduction and application checks were performed and which remain open.

### Refined candidate

Supply positive and reverse treatments, the intended lettering, a restrained
palette, primary and compact arrangements, and examples in the actual contexts.
Record the remaining design decisions. A candidate is not automatically approved.

### Production identity

Supply clean vector masters and useful exports, as the user requests. Include:

- Primary and compact lockups; symbol-only only where it serves a real use.
- Single-color, color, and reverse variants.
- Color values tied to the intended media.
- Minimum sizes and clear space established by inspected applications.
- Type names and provenance; outlined delivery copies where needed, plus editable originals.
- A short usage guide with approved arrangements and relevant misuse examples.

Do not call a raster PNG an SVG, invent print specifications, or declare a logo
print-ready without inspecting an appropriate proof. Archive prior versions
according to the user's instructions.
